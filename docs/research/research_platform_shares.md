Grades: **A** = primary (company filing or official release), **B** = reputable secondary reporting of primary data (MBW, Billboard, trade press quoting MIDiA or Luminate), **C** = estimate, blog, or my own derivation.

**Sanity checks behind the table:**

- **Audio streams.** Spotify says it drives about 70% of on-demand audio streams outside China (Luminate 2025). That puts every other audio service together at about 0.43x Spotify's streams. The audio rows above add up to roughly that.
- **Royalties.** At UMG and WMG, Spotify is about 20% of total company revenue, while recorded-music streaming is about 50% of revenue. All non-Spotify streaming therefore comes to about 1.8–2.0x Spotify. The YouTube, Apple, Amazon and Others rows carry most of that.
- **Per stream.** Non-Spotify audio services earn about 2.5x more per stream than Spotify. This matches Duetti's per-stream ratios (Apple 2.1x, Amazon 2.9x).

## 3. Hip-hop/R&B specifics

- **US genre share.** R&B/hip-hop had 349.9B US on-demand audio streams in 2025, about 25% of the total and still the largest genre. Its share is falling: down 1.7 points in 2024, with another decline in the 2026 midyear report. Sources: [AP via WTOP](https://wtop.com/national/2026/01/music-streams-hit-5-trillion-in-2025-christian-rock-and-latin-lead-growth-in-the-us/), [Luminate blog](https://luminatedata.com/blog/how-rb-hip-hop-streaming-share-could-get-its-groove-back/), [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/lists/luminate-midyear-report-2026/). Grade B.
- **Platform skew.** Mixmag's summary of Luminate's 2025 year-end report says listeners "chose Apple Music and YouTube" for R&B/hip-hop. The report gives no numbers for this ([Mixmag](https://mixmag.net/read/5-trillion-music-streams-2025-report-end-of-year-news)). Grade B/C.
- **Older survey evidence.** Coleman Insights found that hip-hop/R&B made up 48% of Apple Music partisans' Top 100 ([Coleman](https://colemaninsights.com/coleman-insights-blog/slicing-contemporary-music-tastes-by-demographics-and-consumption)). Grade C. This supports raising Apple and YouTube for hip-hop artists.
- **Subscription intent.** Luminate 2025 found R&B/hip-hop fans among the most likely to start a paid subscription in the next six months (MBW, link above). Grade B.
- **TikTok.** Luminate research commissioned by TikTok found that 84% of songs entering the Billboard Global 200 in 2024 went viral on TikTok first ([TikTok newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/tiktok-and-luminate-release-latest-music-impact-report?lang=en)). Grade B, but commissioned. I found **no hip-hop-specific TikTok share**.
- **Audiomack.** Third-party sources describe it as hip-hop and Afrobeats focused; it reports 15.3M MAU in Nigeria. I found **no published hip-hop share of its streams** ([Interspace](https://interspacemusic.com/blog/glossary/audiomack-dsp/), [expression.africa](https://expression.africa/audiomack-celebrates-five-years-in-africa-reaches-58-billion-afrobeats-streams-milestone/)). Grade C.

## 5. YouTube rates

- **Blended rate.** $4.80 per 1,000 streams in 2024 for independent artists, covering YouTube and YouTube Music (Duetti, grade B). This is the most defensible single per-1,000 figure.
- **Art tracks vs Content ID.** Marketing blogs give $5.64 per 1,000 for partner art tracks and $0.87 per 1,000 for general Content ID claims ([Baseform](https://blog.baseformusic.com/articles/ways-to-elevate-your-music-videos-monetization-revealed)). Grade C, not verified against a primary source.
- **Shorts.** Third-party sources estimate about $0.01–0.06 per 1,000 views, and about $0.33 in the US ([Dynamoi](https://dynamoi.com/learn/youtube-music-promotion/youtube-shorts-music-monetization-statistics)). Grade C.
- **Not found.** I found **no label filing that discloses YouTube revenue per view** for official music videos vs Content ID.
- **Recommendation.** You already measure YouTube views, so compute YouTube royalties directly: about $0.6–1.5 per 1,000 for ad-supported official-video views and $4.8 per 1,000 blended (C/B). Use the 0.5–0.75x Spotify ratio only as a cross-check.

## 6. Caveats

1. **Geography.** Every royalty ratio is global, at major-label level. In the US, Apple (31.5% of US subscribers vs 36.9% for Spotify) and Amazon (21.6%) are much larger relative to Spotify than they are globally. Deezer, Tencent, NetEase and Yandex are close to zero for US rap. Shift the mix to match each artist's listener geography.
2. **Hip-hop skew.** Apple and YouTube over-index for hip-hop, but no quantified per-platform genre split is public. The adjustments in section 1 are judgment calls (grade C).
3. **Double counting.** YouTube Music subscription plays of official videos may already be in your measured YouTube views. Add the YouTube Music row only for audio-track (art track) plays.
4. **Masters vs publishing.** The label percentages include publishing revenue from the same DSPs, and the Spotify and YouTube payout totals include publishers. The multipliers apply to master royalties paid to the rights holder. The artist's own share depends on their contract.
5. **Spotify-specific distortions.** Discovery Mode cuts Spotify payouts by 30%, and Spotify has a 1,000-stream minimum per track. Both lower Spotify royalties, so other services look relatively larger. Small artists may be under-paid on Spotify.
6. **Source inconsistencies.**
   - WMG's 10-K cites "697B US on-demand audio streams in 2025, +4.6%". This is probably a midyear figure, since Luminate's year-end total was 1.4T.
   - UMG does not name its three largest customers.
   - The Spotify 70% stream share is self-reported (citing Luminate).
   - Duetti's rates are indie-only, and Spotify disputes them.
7. **Could not verify:**
   - A Luminate or Counterpoint per-service US stream split
   - 2024/2025 Counterpoint subscriber shares
   - Amazon's, TikTok's and Meta's exact share of label revenue in 2025
   - Tidal subscribers and payouts
   - SoundCloud MAU and FPR totals
   - Audiomack payouts and hip-hop share
   - Per-view rates for official videos vs Content ID from a primary source
