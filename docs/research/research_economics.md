# Artist Economics Research: Rate Card, Models and Source Policy
*Compiled 2026-10-23 for a hip-hop artist earnings and benchmarking site. Every figure has a URL. Grades: **A** = primary or official (filings, company disclosures, statutes, court records, Pollstar/Boxscore). **B** = reputable secondary (trade press that reports primary data, vendor datasets with a stated sample, academic or industry studies). **C** = estimate, self-reported claim, or blog.*

> **Main caveat.** No DSP pays a fixed rate per stream. Spotify, Apple, YouTube and Amazon pay each rights holder a share of a revenue pool, based on that holder's share of streams ("streamshare"). Any "per-stream" number is an observed average, and it changes with country, tier and month. The site should show these numbers as **ranges** with their source and year, never as a single precise figure.

**Spotify rules that affect the model (A):**
- From **1 April 2024**, a track must have ≥1,000 streams in the previous 12 months to join the recorded-music royalty pool. There is also an undisclosed unique-listener minimum. Spotify says 99.5% of streams are on tracks that clear the threshold. Sources: [Spotify support](https://support.spotify.com/co-en/artists/article/track-monetization-eligibility/), [MBW](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/changes-to-spotifys-royalty-model-including-the-1000-annual-streams-royalty-policy-are-officially-live). This does not matter for top-100 artists.
- About two-thirds of music revenue goes to rights holders, split roughly 4/5 recording and 1/5 publishing ([Spotify Royalties guide](https://artists.spotify.com/royalties-guide)).
- **Discovery Mode** trades a lower recording royalty for algorithmic promotion. The reported cut is about 30%, which is not verified (C, [Playlist Supply](https://playlistsupply.com/blog/spotify-discovery-mode-explained/)). Any label that uses it lowers the effective rate.

**Spotify Loud & Clear 2026 (covers 2025, A)** ([Spotify newsroom](https://newsroom.spotify.com/?p=39435)):
- Spotify paid out >$11B in 2025 and nearly $70B all-time.
- >1,500 artists generated ≥$1M, 80 generated ≥$10M, and >13,800 generated ≥$100K (+1,400 YoY).
- The 100,000th-ranked artist generated >$7,300.
- About half of royalties went to independent artists and labels.

These are **gross royalties generated for rights holders** (recording plus publishing, before the label's share), not artist take-home pay. The site must say this clearly.

## (b) Revenue models (per artist, per year)

All outputs are **gross income to the artist before personal taxes**, shown as low/mid/high. Each assumption is a parameter the site displays.

### 1. Recorded music (masters)
```
MasterGross_p   = Streams_p × Rate_p           (A1 per platform; country mix if known)
LabelReceipts   = Σ MasterGross_p + physical/downloads (RIAA wholesale ratios)
ArtistMaster    = LabelReceipts × ArtistShare(deal_type)
                  − unrecouped balance (unknown → ignore, flag)
```
**ArtistShare by deal type:**

| Deal type | Low | Mid | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Major royalty deal (new artist) | 15% | 20% | 25% |
| Major royalty deal (superstar/renegotiated) | 20% | 25% | 30% |
| JV / profit split | 30% | 40% | 50% (net-of-costs, so it can approach 50% of receipts once costs are recouped) |
| Label services / AWAL-style | 70% | 80% | 85% |
| DIY distributor | 90% | 100% | 100% |
| Heritage / owns masters | 50% | 50% | 100% |

Sources for these assumptions: CMA, Billboard, AWAL and UnitedMasters (see A3).

**Worked example.** 1B Spotify streams on a major label: 1B × $3.00/1k = $3.0M to the label. The artist gets 20%, about $600K, and then only if recouped. The same streams released DIY return about $3.0M to the artist. This gap is the main thing the site should explain.

**Cross-check.** For reference, Billboard Money Makers deducts 30% from non-US royalties for label holdbacks ([Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/articles/business/money-makers/9602829/musician-global-money-makers-highest-paid-2020-report)).

**Spotify note.** "Loud & Clear" tiers (e.g. ≥$1M generated) count recording **and** publishing gross. Never present them as artist pay.

### 2. Publishing (songwriter)
```
DSP_publishing ≈ MasterGross × 0.25           (Spotify ~1/5 of total pool → pub ≈ 25% of master)
Writer share   = DSP_publishing × writer split on each song (hip-hop: many writers + producer; default 30–50% for the lead artist)
Artist pub net = Writer share × (writer's share 50% + publisher share retained: 0% if full pub deal, 50%+ co-pub, 80-90% admin deal)
+ performance (ASCAP/BMI/SESAC radio, TV) + international + sync (unknown → exclude or C-grade)
```
Use the [Spotify royalty guide](https://artists.spotify.com/royalties-guide) for the pool split and the [CMA](https://gov.uk/government/news/music-streaming-report-published) for publishing's 15% share of UK streaming revenue. Writer splits need credits data (see section 7). **If credits are missing, show the publishing range as low confidence.**

### 3. SoundExchange / neighboring rights
45% of US non-interactive digital radio royalties go to the featured artist. This is small for most artists, so model it as 1–3% of US master gross and grade it C.

### 4. Touring
```
Gross          = Σ shows (Pollstar/Boxscore actual if reported; else capacity × sell-through × avg ticket price)
ArtistGuarantee/Backend ≈ 50–70% of gross for headliner arena/stadium (after venue/promoter costs; promoter keeps ~3% AOI per LYV 10-K)
Artist tour costs (production, crew, travel, band): 25–45% of gross for arena/stadium
Commissions    = agent 10% + manager 15–20% + business manager 5% (of gross/artist fee)
ArtistNet      ≈ Gross × [15% low, 25% mid, 35% high]
```
- Use the reported gross where it exists (A). The net margin is always C.
- Show **gross per show** and **revenue per attendee**. For example, the Grand National Tour's $203.59 average ticket price is 1.5x the Pollstar Top-100 average of $132.62.
- **Highest revenue per fan** should be computed only from Pollstar/Boxscore data (gross ÷ tickets).

### 5. Merch
```
MerchGross = Attendance × per-head (hip-hop $13–15; superstar stadium tours plausibly 1.5–3× — C)
ArtistNet  = MerchGross × (1 − venue cut 0–25%) × (1 − COGS/merch company 40–60%)
```
Add an online/D2C component only when there is a public disclosure (rare).

### 6. Brand deals, features, catalog
- **Brand deals and endorsements:** include only deals that were publicly reported with a figure from a named reputable outlet (C/B). Never extrapolate from influencer rate cards, because celebrity musician deals are bespoke.
- **Features:** do not add fees to income without a source. Show self-reported asking prices as **C context**.
- **Catalog sales:** one-off events. Show them separately and do not mix them into annual earnings. Value = annual NPS × multiple (Citrin Cooperman: about 15x publishing-only, >17x masters or combined).

### 7. Confidence and display
- Compute low/mid/high by propagating parameter ranges (Monte Carlo or simple min/max), and show the **mid with a band**.
- Assign each line item a confidence chip. **High:** A-grade inputs and a known deal type. **Medium:** A-grade volume with an assumed deal. **Low:** any C input.
- Show the **data date** (e.g. "Pollstar 2025 YE; Duetti 2024 rates") on every number.
- Total confidence should equal the lowest confidence of the components that make up more than 20% of the total.

## (d) Recommended source-tier policy

**Tier A (primary, publishable as fact with date):**
- Company filings (SEC 10-K/20-F, UMG/WMG/Sony/Spotify/Live Nation results)
- Statutes and CRB determinations, MLC/ASCAP/BMI/SoundExchange reports
- Court records (PACER, state dockets), and artist-published contracts with images
- Pollstar Boxscore and Billboard Boxscore reported grosses
- DSP official disclosures (Loud & Clear, Apple's letter, YouTube blog)

## Mapping artists to labels (for the deal-type assumption)

1. **℗ line on the release** (the label or owner of the master) vs **© line** (the composition or artwork owner). Reading it:
   - "℗ 2025 Artist LLC under exclusive license to Interscope" means a licensing or JV deal.
   - "℗ Top Dawg Entertainment / Interscope" means an imprint under a major.
   - "℗ Artist, distributed by EMPIRE" means a distribution deal.
   The Spotify Web API album object exposes `label` and `copyrights`. **Caveat:** the February 2026 Dev Mode changes restricted new apps (Premium required, 5-user limit), and some developers report missing `copyrights` and `label` fields. Verify access before relying on this ([Spotify migration guide](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/tutorials/february-2026-migration-guide), [Spotify dev blog](https://developer.spotify.com/blog/2026-02-06-update-on-developer-access-and-platform-security)).
2. **MusicBrainz** (CC0 data): label, imprint and distributor relationships, ISRCs, and writer/producer credits for publishing splits.
3. **Discogs:** label, distributor, ℗/© and "Published By" credits (strong for physical releases, CC0 data dumps).
4. **ISRC registrant code** (characters 3–5 identify the registrant): look it up in IFPI's ISRC search to see who registered the master.
5. **Credits for publishing:** PRO repertoire databases (ASCAP ACE, BMI Repertoire, the Songview joint view) and the MLC public work search name the writers and publishers. These are Tier A for ownership, though not for split percentages in every case.
6. **Deal type comes from press or court records only.** Otherwise infer it from the ℗ wording, flag it as assumed, and widen the range.
